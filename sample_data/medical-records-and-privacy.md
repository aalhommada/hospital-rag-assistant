# Medical records and your privacy

## Seeing your own records

You have the right to see the information the hospital holds about you. This is called a subject access request.

Apply in writing to the medical records department, by post or by email to records@riverside-general.example.nhs.uk. Include your full name, date of birth, address, previous addresses if you have moved, and what you want, whether that is your whole record or a particular clinic letter or scan report.

You must prove your identity. Send a copy of your passport or driving licence and a recent utility bill, or bring the originals to the medical records office on the ground floor of the Blue Zone, open 09:00 to 16:00 on weekdays.

## How long it takes and what it costs

The hospital must respond within one calendar month. If the request is large or complex, this may be extended by a further two months and you will be told why.

Seeing your own records is free. A charge applies only when a request is repetitive or excessive, and you would be told the amount before any work started.

## Requesting records for someone else

You may request another adult's records only with their written consent, or if you hold a lasting power of attorney for health and welfare, or if you have been appointed by a court. Send the consent form or the legal document with your request.

A person with parental responsibility may request the records of a child under thirteen. From thirteen upwards, the child's own view is taken into account, and from sixteen a young person is presumed able to decide for themselves.

For someone who has died, records may be released to the executor of the estate or to a person who may have a claim arising from the death, under the Access to Health Records Act.

## Records for insurance or legal purposes

Requests from solicitors and insurers go through the same office but are charged for, and the fee depends on the size of the record. Telephone 020 7946 0300 for a quotation.

## How your information is used

Your record is used first to care for you. It is also used, in a form that does not identify you, to plan services, to check the quality of care, and for approved research.

Information is shared with your GP as a matter of course. It is shared with other hospitals treating you, and with social care when it is part of your care plan.

We will not share your information for purposes beyond your care without your permission, unless the law requires it, for example a court order or a duty to report certain infectious diseases.

## Opting out

You can stop your confidential information being used for research and planning while still allowing it to be used for your own care. This is called the national data opt-out and is set at nhs.uk/your-nhs-data-matters, or by telephoning 0300 303 5678. It does not affect the care you receive.

## Correcting a mistake

If something in your record is factually wrong, write to the medical records department and say what is wrong and what it should say. A clinical opinion you disagree with cannot be deleted, but your comment will be added to the record so that anyone reading it sees your view alongside it.

## Raising a concern

If you are unhappy with how your information has been handled, contact the Patient Advice and Liaison Service on 020 7946 0250. You may also complain to the Information Commissioner's Office at ico.org.uk.
