# Outpatient appointments and referrals

## How to get an appointment

Most outpatient appointments start with a referral from your GP or from another hospital doctor. The hospital cannot book a specialist appointment for you without one, apart from a small number of self-referral services listed below.

Once a referral arrives, you will be sent an appointment by post or by text message, usually within two weeks.

## Self-referral services

You may book these yourself, with no GP letter: physiotherapy for a musculoskeletal problem, the stop-smoking service, the sexual health clinic, and the antenatal booking clinic. Telephone 020 7946 0100 and say which service you need.

## Changing or cancelling

Telephone the appointments line on 020 7946 0100, Monday to Friday between 08:00 and 18:00. Give at least two working days' notice so the slot can be given to someone else.

An appointment can be rearranged twice. If you miss two appointments without telling us, you may be referred back to your GP and will need a new referral to be seen again.

## If you do not hear anything

Telephone 020 7946 0100 if nothing has arrived four weeks after your GP said they were referring you. Referrals do occasionally go astray, and chasing early is much easier than chasing late.

## On the day

Arrive fifteen minutes before your appointment time and check in at the reception desk for your zone. Bring your appointment letter, your list of medicines, and your reading glasses.

Clinics do run late, most often because an earlier patient needed longer than expected. Allow up to two hours in the department.

You may bring one person with you. Tell reception when you check in if you need an interpreter, a hearing loop, or step-free access, although booking these in advance is much more reliable.

## Video and telephone appointments

Some appointments are offered by video or telephone. You will be sent a link and a joining code by text.

Test your camera and microphone the day before. Join five minutes early. If the connection fails, stay on the line or wait five minutes: the clinician will normally telephone you instead.

Ask for a face-to-face appointment instead if you would prefer one. Nobody has to accept a remote appointment.

## Waiting times

Waiting times differ a great deal by specialty. The appointments line can tell you the current typical wait for the clinic you are waiting for, and where you are on the list.

If your symptoms get worse while you are waiting, contact your GP. Your GP can ask for the referral to be reviewed and made more urgent.

## Results from a clinic

The clinician who saw you will tell you when to expect results and who will contact you. If the time they gave you has passed, telephone the clinic rather than waiting. Ask for the secretary of the consultant you saw; the number is on your appointment letter.

## Feedback and complaints

Speak to the clinic manager on the day if something goes wrong, because most things can be fixed there and then. Otherwise contact the Patient Advice and Liaison Service on 020 7946 0250.
