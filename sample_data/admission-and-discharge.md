# Coming into hospital and going home

## Before you come in

You will usually be invited to a pre-operative assessment one to three weeks before a planned operation. Expect it to last about two hours. A nurse checks your blood pressure, weight, and heart rhythm, takes blood, and goes through every medicine you take, so bring the packets or a printed list from your GP.

Tell the assessment nurse if you take blood-thinning medicine, if you take medicine for diabetes, if you have a heart pacemaker, or if you have ever had a bad reaction to an anaesthetic.

## What to bring for an overnight stay

Bring nightwear, a dressing gown, non-slip slippers, and a towel. Bring your own toothbrush, soap, and shampoo. Bring all your medicines in their original boxes.

Bring a small amount of cash for newspapers, and a phone with a charger. Leave jewellery, bank cards, and anything valuable at home. The hospital cannot accept responsibility for belongings kept at the bedside.

## Fasting before an operation

Unless your letter says otherwise, do not eat for six hours before the time you are told to arrive, and drink no fluids except water for two hours before. Water is allowed up to two hours before.

Take your normal medicines with a small sip of water on the morning of the operation, unless the pre-operative assessment nurse told you to stop something.

## On the day

Report to the surgical admissions lounge on level 1 of the Blue Zone at the time on your letter, which will be earlier than your operation time. Several people are admitted for the same list, so the order is decided by the surgical team on the day and cannot be requested.

One person may wait with you in the lounge until you go to theatre.

## While you are on the ward

Ward rounds happen every morning, usually between 08:00 and 11:00. This is the best time to ask the medical team questions, so write them down the night before.

Meals are served at 08:00, 12:30, and 17:30. Tell the ward if you have a special diet, an allergy, or a religious requirement, and the catering team will arrange it.

## Going home

Discharge is aimed at before midday, but the actual time depends on the doctor's review, your medicines being dispensed by the pharmacy, and transport. Waiting for take-home medicines is the most common cause of delay and can add two to three hours.

You will be given a discharge summary explaining what was done and what happens next. A copy is sent to your GP within twenty-four hours.

Arrange for someone to collect you. If you have had an anaesthetic or sedation, you must not drive yourself and a responsible adult must be with you for the first twenty-four hours.

## The discharge lounge

If your bed is needed and you are otherwise ready, you may be moved to the discharge lounge on the ground floor of the Blue Zone to wait comfortably. There are armchairs, refreshments, and a nurse present.

## After you go home

You will be told who to contact if you are worried. For most surgical patients this is the ward you stayed on, at any hour, for the first two weeks. Keep the number on your discharge summary somewhere you can find it.

Follow-up appointments are usually posted within two weeks. If nothing has arrived after three weeks, telephone outpatient appointments on 020 7946 0100.
