# Preparing for a colonoscopy

A colonoscopy is an examination of the large bowel using a thin flexible camera. The preparation matters more than the procedure: if the bowel is not clear, the examination has to be repeated.

## Three days before

Stop eating nuts, seeds, sweetcorn, wholegrain bread, brown rice, and raw vegetables and fruit skins. These leave residue that blocks the camera's view.

If you take iron tablets, stop them seven days before. If you take blood-thinning medicine such as warfarin, apixaban, or clopidogrel, telephone the Endoscopy unit on 020 7946 0188 at least two weeks before, because you may need to stop or change the dose and only your doctor can tell you how.

## The day before

Eat a light breakfast and a light lunch of plain foods such as white bread, eggs, or plain chicken. Do not eat anything after 14:00.

Take the bowel preparation sachets exactly as written in the leaflet in your pack, usually one in the afternoon and one in the evening. Drink at least two litres of clear fluid on top of the preparation itself. Clear fluid means water, black tea or coffee without milk, clear apple juice, or clear broth. Nothing red or purple.

Stay near a toilet. The preparation works within one to three hours and continues for several hours.

## The day of the procedure

You may drink water until two hours before your appointment time. Nothing at all in the last two hours.

Bring your appointment letter, your list of medicines, and a dressing gown or loose clothing. Leave valuables at home.

## Sedation and going home

Most people choose light sedation. If you have sedation you must not drive, operate machinery, sign legal documents, or drink alcohol for twenty-four hours afterwards, and a responsible adult must collect you from the unit and stay with you overnight. We will cancel the procedure on the day if nobody is available to take you home, so please arrange this before you come.

If you choose to have the procedure without sedation, you may drive yourself home.

## How long it takes

The procedure itself takes twenty to forty minutes. Allow four hours in the unit in total, including recovery time.

## Afterwards

Bloating and wind are normal for a few hours. Eat and drink normally once you feel ready.

Contact the unit or go to the Emergency department if you have severe abdominal pain, heavy bleeding from the back passage, or a fever above 38 degrees in the two weeks after your procedure.
