# Visiting hours and ward rules

Visitors are welcome. The rules below exist so that patients can rest and so that staff can work safely, and they differ by ward.

## General wards

General wards are open to visitors from 14:00 to 16:00 and again from 18:00 to 20:00, seven days a week. Two visitors per patient at a time. If more people wish to visit, please take turns and wait in the ground floor cafe rather than on the ward.

## Intensive care unit

The intensive care unit on level 3 is open from 11:00 to 19:00. Only two named visitors per patient are allowed, and they must be agreed with the nurse in charge when the patient is admitted. Telephone the unit on 020 7946 0203 before you travel, because visiting is sometimes paused during ward rounds or emergencies.

## Maternity

The birth partner may stay at any time, including overnight. Other visitors, including brothers and sisters of the new baby, may come between 15:00 and 19:00.

## Children's ward

One parent or guardian may stay overnight at the bedside, and a fold-down bed is provided. Other visitors are welcome from 12:00 to 19:00. Visitors under sixteen must be accompanied by an adult.

## Outside visiting hours

If someone is seriously ill or at the end of their life, visiting restrictions do not apply. Speak to the nurse in charge of the ward, who can arrange access at any hour.

## Children as visitors

Children are welcome but must stay with an adult at all times and must not visit if they are unwell.

## What you may bring

Fresh fruit, sealed snacks, books, magazines, headphones, and a phone charger are all welcome. Please keep the bedside table clear so that staff can reach the patient.

Do not bring flowers. Flowers are not allowed on any ward because of infection risk and because pollen affects other patients.

Do not bring latex balloons, large bags, or electrical items that have not been checked by our estates team.

## Food from outside

You may bring food in, but tell the nurse first. Some patients are fasting before a procedure, on a restricted diet, or being weighed as part of their treatment. There is a fridge on each ward with a shelf for patients' own food, and items must be labelled with the patient's name and the date.

## Infection control

Clean your hands with the gel at the ward entrance when you arrive and again when you leave.

Do not visit if you have had diarrhoea or vomiting in the last forty-eight hours, or if you have a fever, a new cough, flu, chickenpox, or shingles. Telephone the ward instead and we will pass on your message.

## Mobile phones and photographs

Mobile phones may be used quietly in patient areas. Please do not take photographs or record video anywhere in the hospital, because other patients are in the background and have not agreed to it.
