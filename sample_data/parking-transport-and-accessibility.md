# Parking, transport, and accessibility

## Car parks

Car park A, in front of the main entrance, is for visitors and outpatients. Car park B, off Mill Lane, is for the Emergency department and is closest to the Amber Zone. Car park C, at the rear, is for staff only and is barrier-controlled.

Car parks A and B are open twenty-four hours. Spaces are hardest to find between 09:00 and 11:00 on weekday mornings.

## Parking charges

Charges apply between 07:00 and 20:00. Up to thirty minutes is free. Up to one hour costs three pounds, up to two hours costs five pounds, up to four hours costs eight pounds, and up to twenty-four hours costs twelve pounds. Parking is free between 20:00 and 07:00 and all day on Sundays.

Pay at the machines in either car park, or by card at the machine in the main entrance foyer, before you return to your car. The machines take cards and contactless payment. Car park A machines do not take cash.

## Reduced and free parking

Parking is free for blue badge holders in the marked bays. Display your badge.

A weekly pass costing fifteen pounds is available from the main reception desk for relatives visiting a patient who is staying more than three days, and for patients attending frequent treatment such as dialysis or chemotherapy. Ask the ward clerk for a letter, then take it to reception.

Parents staying overnight with a child on the children's ward park free. Ask the ward for a validation ticket.

## Blue badge and accessible parking

There are twenty-two blue badge bays: fourteen in car park A close to the main entrance and eight in car park B. They are wider than standard bays and have a dropped kerb onto the pavement.

## Getting here without a car

Buses 41, 108, and 262 stop directly outside the main entrance on Riverside Road. The 41 runs every ten minutes during the day.

The nearest railway station is Riverside Central, about twelve minutes on foot, or one stop on the 41 bus.

There is a taxi rank outside the main entrance. The hospital does not book taxis for patients, but reception can give you the number of a local firm.

## Non-emergency patient transport

Free transport is available if a medical condition means you cannot travel to your appointment any other way. It is not available simply because you have no car or no lift.

Telephone the patient transport booking line on 020 7946 0400 at least three working days before your appointment. You will be asked a short set of questions about your mobility to check whether you are eligible. An escort can travel with you if you need help during the journey.

## Inside the hospital

Every zone has step-free access from the main entrance. Lifts serve all levels in all four zones.

Wheelchairs are available free of charge at the main entrance; a one pound refundable coin deposit is needed to release them.

Accessible toilets are on every floor of every zone. Hearing loops are fitted at the main reception desk, at outpatient reception, and in every clinic room.

## Interpreters and communication support

Interpreting is free and available in more than one hundred languages, including British Sign Language. Book it in advance by telephoning the outpatient appointments line on 020 7946 0100 at least three working days before your appointment, or ask the ward clerk if you are an inpatient. Telephone interpreting can be arranged within minutes in an emergency.

Please do not rely on a family member to interpret clinical information. A trained interpreter is free, and using one protects you.

## Assistance dogs

Registered assistance dogs are welcome everywhere in the hospital except operating theatres, the intensive care unit, and the neonatal unit. Tell the ward or clinic in advance so a water bowl and a quiet space can be arranged.
