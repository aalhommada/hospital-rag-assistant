# Billing, insurance, and payments

## NHS patients

If you are entitled to NHS treatment, your hospital care is free at the point of use. This includes outpatient appointments, tests, operations, and inpatient stays.

Things that are not part of NHS treatment and are charged for include car parking, the television and telephone service at the bedside, and copies of medical records requested for insurance or legal purposes.

## Establishing entitlement

The overseas visitors team may write to you before or after your appointment to check whether you are entitled to free NHS care. You may be asked for a passport, a residence permit, proof of your address, and a European or global health insurance card if you hold one.

Reply within fourteen days. Not replying does not cancel your care, but it may mean you are invoiced for it.

## Private and insured patients

If you are paying yourself or through insurance, contact the private patients office on 020 7946 0500 before your appointment.

For insured patients, get written authorisation from your insurer first and bring the authorisation code and your policy number to your appointment. Without an authorisation code we will treat the episode as self-funded and invoice you directly.

You will be sent a written estimate before any planned procedure. The estimate covers the hospital's charges. Fees for the consultant and the anaesthetist are billed separately by them, and are not included, which surprises people often enough that it is worth repeating.

## Paying an invoice

Invoices are payable within twenty-eight days. Pay by bank transfer using the details on the invoice, by card on 020 7946 0505, or in person at the cashier's office on the ground floor of the Green Zone, open 09:00 to 16:00 on weekdays.

Always quote the invoice number. Payments without a reference can take several weeks to match to an account.

## If you cannot pay

Telephone the accounts team on 020 7946 0505 as soon as you know there is a problem. Instalment plans can be arranged, and doing this early is far better than waiting for a reminder letter.

## Help with travel costs

If you receive Income Support, Universal Credit, Pension Credit Guarantee Credit, or hold an HC2 or HC3 certificate, you may be able to claim back the cost of travelling to your appointment.

Bring your appointment letter, proof of the benefit you receive, and your travel receipts to the cashier's office on the day of your appointment. Claims made on the day are paid in cash. Claims made later must be posted and take up to four weeks.

## Questions about a bill

Contact the accounts team on 020 7946 0505 or by email to accounts@riverside-general.example.nhs.uk. If you believe an invoice is wrong, say so in writing within twenty-eight days and the charge will be held while it is reviewed.
